Understanding mental wellness

Mood

What is depression?

Depression is more than sadness. It's a persistent low mood affecting energy, thinking, and daily functioning. It's common and treatable.

Self-esteem

Building self-worth

Self-esteem is how you evaluate yourself. Low self-esteem can be improved through consistent practice and sometimes professional support.

Wellness

Emotional resilience

Resilience is the ability to adapt in the face of adversity. It can be built — it's not a fixed trait you either have or don't.

Stress

Understanding anxiety

Anxiety is a normal response to stress, but when persistent, it can interfere with life. Effective evidence-based treatments exist.

Self-help practices

Mindfulness

5-4-3-2-1 grounding

Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Helps interrupt anxious thoughts in the moment.

Breathing

Box breathing

Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat 4 times. Activates the parasympathetic nervous system to reduce stress quickly.

Journaling

Expressive writing

Writing about thoughts and feelings for 15–20 minutes has strong evidence for improving mood and processing difficult emotions.

Movement

Exercise & mood

Even 20–30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise 3x per week has significant evidence for improving mood and reducing anxiety.

When to seek professional help

Consider reaching out to a professional if you experience:

  • Persistent low mood lasting more than two weeks
  • Thoughts of harming yourself or others
  • Significant changes in sleep, appetite, or weight
  • Difficulty functioning at work, school, or in relationships
  • Feeling hopeless or that life is not worth living
  • Using substances to cope with emotional pain
  • Anxiety that feels uncontrollable or overwhelming
  • Feeling disconnected from yourself or reality
